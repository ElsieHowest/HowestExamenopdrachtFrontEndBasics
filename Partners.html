Partners and inspiration

Surfing is more than a sport: it’s freedom, nature, and connection.

I started surfing at 16 during summer camp in France. What began as something a friend introduced me to quickly grew into a passion I could not imagine my life without.

Surfing has taught me patience and resilience. The ocean does not care about your schedule or your mood. You fall, you swallow water, you miss waves, and you learn to get back up anyway. That mindset slowly found its way into other parts of my life, from how I deal with setbacks to how I approach new challenges.

Surfing is as much a mental practice as it is a physical one.

Physically, surfing builds strength, balance and endurance in a way that rarely feels like "training" — you are too focused on the wave to notice you are working your entire body. Mentally, it forces you to slow down. Out on the water, there is no room for overthinking; you have to be fully present, reading the ocean, reacting in the moment. That combination of physical effort and mental presence is, for me, what makes surfing such a powerful reset for both body and mind.

This personal connection to surfing is exactly why I wanted this exam project to feel authentic rather than purely commercial. Salty Cheeks is not just a concept of a shop to me — it is a reflection of what surfing has given me, and a way to imagine sharing that with others.

These references were used for inspiration, not imitation.

During the design process, I looked at different surf brands, surf clubs, and surf shops. This helped me understand how strong visual identities and brand experiences are created, especially through colour, typography, and product presentation.

The brands I used as references are either ones I personally use or have visited during surf trips. Surfing has had a big influence on my personal development, so this project also gave me the chance to reflect that in my own design approach.

These references were used as inspiration only, not for copying. They helped me make clearer design choices and build a more structured and thoughtful concept.

Surfing has strongly influenced my personal development.

Lastly, I want to share a small idea behind this project. I often imagine this surf shop evolving into a future app for learning and connecting with other surfers. Surfing can be difficult to learn, and an online tool could make it more accessible in Belgium.

More women are finding their way into surfing, and I would like to support that shift. A platform where learning and meeting others come together feels like a natural next step. Not only for women, but for anyone who has ever thought it would be amazing to learn how to surf.
